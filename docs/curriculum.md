# Curriculum: Utah 3rd grade, 2026–27

This doc says what Utah expects a 3rd grader to know this year, how the state checks it, and where each piece lives in the game. The game's content generators, the tutor's hints and the grown-ups corner are all built from it.

## Which standards apply this year

| Subject | Standards in force for 2026–27 | Status | State test in grade 3? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Math | Utah Core Standards for Mathematics K–5 (adopted 2010, **revised April 2016**) | Current. New standards were drafted but paused in January 2026, so the earliest change is 2027–28. | **RISE Math** (spring) |
| English language arts (ELA) | Utah State Standards for P–12 ELA (**adopted May 2023**) | Current | **RISE ELA, reading only.** RISE tests writing only in grades 5 and 8 |
| Science | Utah SEEd (2019 text, unchanged in the 2023 document) | Current | None (RISE Science starts in grade 4) |
| Social studies | Utah Core Standards, Elementary Social Studies (**December 2022**) | Current. A review under the 2026 law HB 312 will add founding-document content by 2028–29. | None |
| Also | K–5 Computer Science (2019), Health (2019), Library Media digital citizenship | Light touches only | None |

**Code formats.** The game tags every item with these codes:

- **Math** `3.OA.7`, `3.OA.7.b`. No cluster letters; Common Core's `3.OA.C.7` is the same standard.
- **ELA** `3.R.3`, `3.R.3.b`, `3.W.3`, `3.SL.1`. Since 2023 there is no separate Language strand; grammar, spelling and vocabulary are folded into Reading and Writing.
- **Science** `SEEd 3.2.4`.
- **Social studies** `SS 3.3.6`.

## How Utah checks whether he's on track

| When (state window) | Check | On track looks like |
|---|---|---|
| First day of school – Sep 30, 2026 | **Acadience Reading**, beginning of year | Reading Composite **≥ 220** (above benchmark ≥ 289). Oral reading about **70+ words correct per minute** at 95%+ accuracy. |
| Dec 1, 2026 – Jan 31, 2027 | **Acadience Reading**, middle of year | Composite **≥ 285** (above ≥ 349). About **86+ words per minute** at 96%+. |
| Apr 15 – Jun 15, 2027 | **Acadience Reading**, end of year | Composite **≥ 330**, which is "reading on grade level" under the 2026 law SB 241 (above benchmark ≥ 405). About **100+ words per minute** at 97%+. |
| Spring 2027 (most districts test in April–May) | **RISE ELA** (reading) and **RISE Math** | **Level 3 (Proficient)** or **Level 4 (Highly Proficient)** |
| Fall, winter and spring, if the school still gives it | **Acadience Math** (optional statewide this year) | End of year: Computation ≥ 29, Concepts & Applications ≥ 47 |
| Each term | District report card | Usually a **3** ("Proficient") on each standard |

What the RISE tests look like:

- Online, computer-adaptive and untimed, about 90–135 minutes per subject.
- No calculator in grades 3–5.
- Item types: multiple choice, multi-select, hot text (click or drag words in a passage), drag-and-drop, grid items (plot points, draw shapes), table match and equation entry.
- **Grade 3 math blend:** operations and algebraic thinking 29–38%, **fractions 27–31%**, base-ten 18–22%, measurement, data and geometry 18–22%.
- **Grade 3 ELA:** reading passages only. Writing conventions and speaking and listening are no longer tested.
- **Free practice tests:** [utahrise.org/training-tests.html](https://utahrise.org/training-tests.html). Sign in as a guest.

**For context:** in spring 2025, 70.5% of Utah 3rd graders were at or above the Acadience benchmark, and 44.7% were proficient on RISE ELA. In 2026, 50.1% were proficient on RISE Math.

### Worth asking his school now

1. **His Acadience Reading results from this fall** (composite, words per minute, accuracy). Under SB 241, schools must share results after every benchmark. That's the game's reading baseline.
2. **Whether the school gives Acadience Math this year**, and his fall results if so.
3. **The math curriculum and its order of units**, so crystals unlock in step with class. The state publishes no pacing guide.
4. **The weekly spelling list.**
5. **The district's RISE window** (usually April–May).

## How the game measures mastery

- **Skills under standards.** Each standard splits into skills. For example, 3.NBT.2 splits into: add without regrouping, add regrouping ones, add regrouping tens, subtract regrouping tens, subtract regrouping hundreds, subtract across zeros. Every problem is tagged with its skill and standard.
- **What each attempt records:** right or wrong, tier, hints used, answer time (quiet, never shown), and the mistake pattern if wrong.
- **Levels per skill**, shown as the Crystal Grid to him and as a checklist to you:

| Level | Rule |
|---|---|
| Not started | No attempts yet |
| Learning | Recent accuracy below 70% |
| Practicing | 70–89% |
| **On track** | 90%+ over the last 10 or more attempts, spread over 3+ days, without hints |
| **Mastered** | On track, *and* still 90%+ on review two or more weeks later. For facts that must be "from memory" (3.OA.7.b), the typical answer also comes in about 3 seconds without a picture. |

- **Spaced review.** Mastered skills come back after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. A skill that slips drops a level. This follows USBE's own fluency advice: focus practice on facts that are "understood but not yet fluent."
- **A standard's status** is the status of its weakest core skill.
- **Bosses are unit checks.** A boss's final phase allows no hints, and its result is logged as a check on the standards for that chapter.
- **Reading fluency (later):** an optional one-minute read-aloud, scored like Acadience's oral reading, by speech recognition or timed by you.
- **Writing:**
  - Opinion and informative pieces are scored on the RISE grades 3–5 traits: purpose and organization, evidence and elaboration, conventions.
  - Narratives use the 3.W.3 checklist below.
  - The tutor drafts the feedback and you see everything.

## Math

### Limits the problem generators must respect

| Area | Grade 3 limit |
|---|---|
| Multiply and divide | Within 100. Whole-number answers only, so **no remainders** (those come in 4th grade). |
| Facts from memory | All products of two one-digit numbers, 0–9 × 0–9, by the end of the year. ×10 is practiced as a pattern. |
| Add and subtract | Within 1,000. **Any valid strategy is accepted. The standard "carry/borrow" algorithm is not required in grade 3** (it's expected in 4th). |
| Word-problem types | No "times as many" comparison problems (4th grade) |
| Fractions | **Denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 only.** Area, bar and number-line models only, **no sets** ("3 of 4 apples" is 4th grade). Fractions greater than 1 are allowed. |
| Rounding | To the nearest 10 or 100. A 5 rounds up. |
| Multiples of 10 | One-digit × 10–90 (9 × 80) |
| Time | To the nearest minute; elapsed time in minutes |
| Mass and liquid volume | g, kg, mL, L. One-step problems in the same units. No cm³. |
| Area | sq cm, sq m, sq in, sq ft, and made-up units |
| Rulers | Marked in ½ and ¼ inch |
| Shapes | Utah's **inclusive trapezoid** (at least one pair of parallel sides), so never mark "a parallelogram is a trapezoid" wrong |
| Arrays | Accept rows × columns *or* columns × rows |

### Mistake patterns the tutor watches for

Built from Utah's Core Guides and common 3rd-grade error research. When a wrong answer matches a pattern, the droid gives the lesson for *that* mistake.

| Standard | Wrong answer | What it means | Mini-lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.NBT.2 | 352 − 128 → **236** | Took the smaller digit from the bigger in each column | Trade a ten for ten ones (base-ten blocks) |
| 3.NBT.2 | 352 − 128 → **234** | Traded a ten but didn't take it away from the tens | Track the traded ten |
| 3.NBT.2 | 403 − 156 → **353** | Stuck at the zero | A hundred → ten tens → one ten for the ones |
| 3.NBT.2 | 456 + 278 → **624** | Didn't make a new ten or hundred | Ten ones make a ten |
| 3.NBT.2 | 405 + 37 → **775** | Lined up the wrong places | Line up ones with ones |
| 3.OA.7 | 7 × 8 → **54, 48, 63** | Mixed up a neighboring fact | Break apart: 7 × 8 = 7 × 4 + 7 × 4 |
| 3.OA.7 | 7 × 8 → **49, 64** | Off by one group | Skip-count from a fact he knows |
| 3.OA.1 | 5 × 7 → **12** | Added the numbers | Picture of equal groups |
| 3.OA.7 | 7 × 0 → **7**; 7 × 1 → **8** | Zero and one rules mixed up | "7 groups of nothing" |
| 3.OA.4 | 8 × ? = 48 → **384** or **56** | Did math on the numbers he could see | ? is a missing factor: 8 times *what*? |
| 3.OA.2 | 12 ÷ 3 → **9** | Subtracted once instead of sharing | Deal into equal groups |
| 3.OA.8 | Answer = the first step's result | Stopped halfway | Two-step bar model |
| 3.OA.8 | 3 + 4 × 2 → **14** | Worked left to right | Multiply before adding |
| 3.NBT.1 | 478 → **470** (nearest 10) | Chopped instead of rounding | Number line: which ten is closer? |
| 3.NBT.1 | 448 → **500** (nearest 100) | Rounded twice in a row | Round once, from the original number |
| 3.NBT.3 | 5 × 60 → **30** or **3000** | Dropped or added a zero | 5 × 6 tens = 30 tens |
| 3.NF.1 | 3 of 4 parts shaded → **3/1, 1/4, 4/3** | Mixed up parts and whole | Name the whole first |
| 3.NF.3 | **2/6 > 2/4** | Bigger number means bigger fraction | More pieces means smaller pieces |
| 3.NF.2 | First mark of 4 parts → **1/5** | Counted tick marks, not spaces | Count the jumps |
| 3.MD.1 | 2:50 read as **3:50** | The hour hand is *near* 3 but not past it | Where the hour hand really is |
| 3.MD.1 | 9:45 + 30 min → **9:75** | Treated time like base 10 | 60 minutes in an hour |
| 3.MD.7 | Area of 3 × 5 → **8** | Added the sides | Tile it |
| 3.MD.8 | Perimeter of 3 × 5 → **8** or **12** | Added two sides, or counted border squares | Walk all four sides |
| 3.MD.3 | 4 symbols with a key of 5 → **4** | Ignored the key | Each picture means 5 |
| 3.G.1 | "A square isn't a rectangle" | Category mix-up | Check shared features |

## English language arts

### Spelling and word study (his Spellwright): 3.R.2–3

Grade 3 is **the last year Utah lists spelling patterns explicitly**, so this year matters.

| Code | Expectation | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| 3.R.2.a–b | Phonological awareness: reverse sounds; add, delete or swap a sound anywhere in a word | **Transmute:** change one sound to turn a fiend's word into a harmless one (*beast → feast → least*) |
| 3.R.3.a | Read unfamiliar multisyllable words using sounds, syllables, roots and affixes | Ruin inscriptions, Lost Primers, the reading in every scene |
| 3.R.3.b | **Read and spell all six syllable types** in longer words: closed (*rab-bit*), open (*ro-bot*), silent-e (*stam-pede*), vowel team (*rain-coat*), vowel-r (*thun-der*), consonant-le (*bat-tle*) | Spell scrolls grouped by syllable type |
| 3.R.3.c | Common prefixes and suffixes (USBE examples: *pre-, re-, dis-, im-, in-, -tion; -s, -es, -ies, -ing, -ed, -er, -or, -est*) | **Affix runes**, including the spelling rules for adding them (double the consonant, drop the e, y → i) |
| 3.R.3.d | The unaccented "schwa" syllable when reading and spelling (*nation, active, atomic*) | **Hidden-sound words**: the tricky middle syllables |
| 3.R.3.e | **Read and spell common irregular words** | **Wild words**, which must be memorized; drilled in Word Storm |
| Review from grade 2 | Schwa (*ago, again*); compound words; *ph/gh/ch*; *tch/dge*; hard and soft *c/g*; silent letters (*kn, wr, mb*); *oi/oy, ou/ow, au/aw*; *-old, -ild, -ind* | Diagnostic spell scrolls in chapter 1 to find gaps |
| His school list | Whatever comes home each week | That week's spell scrolls and the Thursday Spell Boss |

### Writing: 3.W.1–5 (not on grade-3 RISE, but his biggest growth area)

| Code | Expectation | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| 3.W.1 | Opinion/argument: claim, evidence, linking words, conclusion | Persuade the council; argue which Titan would survive on the ice island |
| 3.W.2 | Informative: topic, facts and details grouped together, linking words, conclusion | **Bestiary field notes**; Titan habitat reports |
| 3.W.3 | **Narrative** (see the checklist below) | **Titan entrances**, tiered story choices, the camp journal, "Tales" side quests |
| 3.W.1–3 b and c | Write and expand simple, compound and complex sentences; conventions | **Cleric**: Restore (expand sentences), cure ailments (punctuation and capitals) |
| 3.W.4 | Short research projects with notes | **Scholar** field research: read two sources, take notes, report |
| 3.W.5 | Handwriting, cursive and print | Off-screen. Optional: copy his best Titan entrance by hand into a paper Titan Codex. |

**The 3.W.3 narrative checklist,** which the tutor uses for feedback and the Crystal Grid uses for writing nodes:

1. Set the scene.
2. Introduce the narrator or characters.
3. Events unfold in a natural order.
4. Descriptive details.
5. Dialogue.
6. Characters' actions, thoughts and feelings.
7. A mix of simple, compound and complex sentences.
8. Conventions.
9. A concluding statement.

Story Quest's picture test (specific, vivid detail) carries over for descriptions.

### Speaking and listening: 3.SL.1–3

| Code | Expectation | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| 3.SL.1 | Conversations: respond to others, ask questions, stay on topic | **Talk it out** with the droid; Wit Duels as playful back-and-forth |
| 3.SL.2 | Ask and answer clearly about a topic | Voice mode: explain his math thinking out loud |
| 3.SL.3 | Clear language; present a story or opinion in a sensible order | Present his new Titan to the crew, out loud, before it can join |

**Grammar, briefly.** The 2023 standards dropped the old itemized grammar list: parts of speech, plurals, verb tenses, agreement, comparatives, conjunctions and quotation marks. The Cleric still uses that list as an optional checklist. What Utah requires is in 3.W.b and c: expanding sentences and using conventions (capitals, end punctuation, commas, descriptive words, spelling).

## Social studies: Utah 2022 (22 standards, not state-tested)

The grade-3 theme is "your own community, compared with the world." The fantasy islands make the ideas concrete. The real-world parts (his actual town, the real U.S. and Utah Constitutions) come through short **Envoy missions** done with you. This strand is under review for 2028–29, so it stays modular.

| Strand | Standards | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Government and constitutions | 3.1.1 forms of government (constitutional republic, pure democracy, oligarchy, monarchy); 3.1.2 "We the People"; 3.1.3 the Constitution as supreme law; 3.1.4 First Amendment rights; 3.1.5 checks and balances | Islands with different governments: the Coral Queen's monarchy, a pirate crew that votes on everything (real pirates did!), the Merchant Guild's oligarchy, and the Free Port's charter with three branches that check each other. Real-world mission: read the Preamble together. |
| 2. Your community | 3.2.1 find town, state, country and continent on maps; 3.2.2 geography shaped it; 3.2.3 its cultures; 3.2.4 how it changed | Each island's geography shapes its town. Real-world missions: find his town on a map, and one way it has changed. |
| 3. Rights and responsibilities | 3.3.1 the people who shaped it, primary vs. secondary sources; 3.3.2 decisions in groups vs. democracy; 3.3.3 improvements over time; 3.3.4 civic roles; 3.3.5 how leaders are chosen; 3.3.6 taxes; 3.3.7 choices that shape the future; 3.3.8 a service project | An old captain's log (primary) vs. a history book (secondary); crew votes; the harbor master and fire brigade; the port's tax collector (taxes pay for the lighthouse); a family service project as a real-world mission |
| 4. Connecting to the world | 3.4.1–3.4.5: pick a community outside the U.S.; map it, then its geography, culture, government and adaptations | He researches a real place (3.W.4 too), for example Japan, home of the kaiju, and **it inspires a new island in the game** |

## Light touches

- **Computer science 3.AP.1** (sequences, loops, events, conditionals): **droid protocol** puzzles. The data standards 3.DA.1–2 overlap with the Ranger's graphs.
- **Digital citizenship and health:** the droid never asks for personal information, and an in-story moment covers what to do online when something feels wrong (Library Media grades 3–4; Health 3.SDP.3).
