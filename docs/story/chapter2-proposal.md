# Chapter 2: Driftwood Harbor (proposal)

## Decisions (13 October)

Andy approved the outline ("all sounds good"), after shaping it in the project's chapter 2 thread:

- **Five heroes.** Tamsin Hollow, the Beastkeeper, joins as the fifth. She counters a new kind of fiend, **fast fiends**, with Slow (elapsed time), and her Feed command uses grams and liters. She turns captured fiends into **pets** for the other heroes. Cade's first is Rivet, a rebuilt Scrap Raptor.
- **A new villain:** Inspector Clamp of the Iron Dominion, the Dominion's first face. Jumble stays comic chaos, with **one serious moment** that makes him human (beat 9b).
- **Maren is special, the way the summoner is in FFX.** She gets a second Titan, **Beaconwing**, and a second original song, the **Lantern Song** (lyrics approved).
- **The upgrade map is the Crystal Grid,** close to FFX's Sphere Grid: light from fights moves each hero along their own path, and some nodes are gates that open by mastering a skill. Every fight pays out on it, so the grind always moves something forward.
- **Weapons:** one signature weapon per hero, upgraded in two steps at the Sword Master's forge. Legendary Weapons stay the season-long hunt.
- **Going back pays:** once the crystal is nearly home, the island remembers, and old places change (beat 10).
- **The shape:** two days and a night, with the lighthouse at night in the middle. Tamsin is the keeper's granddaughter. Kit gets the hat back, then loses it again, funnily, and wears Hal's tea cozy.
- **Built separately, for chapter 1 too** (their own threads and pull requests): a skill level per child with turn-swapping when siblings play together; an optional play timer that ends at camp; real misses for the wrong hero and on-screen badges for the right one.

The pictures are in `art/waves/wave-08-harbor.md`. The script comes next, in `docs/story/chapter2-script.md`. Nothing in the game has changed yet.

## Summary

**The story in one breath.** The morning after the Geode Titan, the party carries the new crystal down to Driftwood Harbor. The town has gone quiet: no bells, no songs, sums gone wrong, recipe books gone blank. Two villains arrive the same morning: Captain Jumble, who scrambles the clock tower to keep the town confused, and Inspector Clamp of the Iron Dominion, who seals off the lighthouse "for inventory" and parks a draining machine on it. The party finds Tamsin, a harbor kid freeing the fiends caught inside Clamp's drones, and she joins as the fifth hero. She shows them that a fiend can be *remembered* into a friend, and Cade gets Rivet. Sneaking into the lighthouse by night, they find the old keeper's logbook and learn why he climbs every night: a Titan sleeps in the lens, and he keeps it company. The old crystal's last light throws the Crystal Grid across the ceiling. At dusk on the second day, Clamp's Drain Engine clamps onto the tower. Maren calls Beaconwing awake, it tears the Engine off, the new crystal goes in, and the harbor remembers. The bells ring.

**What's new, and where it arrives:**

| New thing | Where it arrives |
|---|---|
| Tamsin, the fifth hero | Beat 3, freeing caged drones |
| Pets | Beat 4, Rivet for Cade. More as Tamsin grows (beat 9 and later) |
| The Crystal Grid | Beat 7, the lamp room. It opens early, so the grind always pays |
| Inspector Clamp | Beat 1, fining Jumble on the dock |
| Beaconwing, Maren's second Titan | Beat 11, the boss |
| New fiends | All over the harbor, each with a pet line |
| Rewards in old places | Beats 6 and 10: the cove's sea cave, the temple's library, the canyon's parts |
| The forge | Beat 8, the Sword Master's dock |

**The heart of the chapter (the Maren's-song bar):** the keeper's logbook in beat 7. An old man who forgets everything wrote one line in it every night so he'd remember: *"Said goodnight to the bird."* The player reads it, nobody explains it, and the next night Beaconwing wakes to the keeper's voice.

**Time.** Two days and a night: the harbor by day, the lighthouse at night, and the boss at the second dusk. It's a bigger chapter than the first, which fits December (and winter break).

## The beats

The learning in each beat uses December's class topics (`docs/curriculum.md`): times-table facts with 6 to 9, multiples of 10, time to the minute and elapsed time, grams and liters, prefixes and suffixes, and informative writing. The chapter 1 subjects keep going in battle.

### 1. Into the harbor (morning)
- The map trail to the harbor opens. The party walks into a silent town: laundry hanging still, a bell tower with no bells ringing, a fiddler staring at his fiddle.
- **The clock tower's hands are scrambled.** Jumble moored his galleon overnight and spun them. A town that can't read its own clock misses its tides. **Puzzle:** set the tower right from the tide board (time to the minute).
- **Inspector Clamp arrives** in a Dominion cutter, stamps the dock and writes Jumble a parking fine for the *Misspelled* ("loading zone"). Jumble is outraged. Two villains who can't stand each other, instantly.
  - `[politely] Inspector Clamp, Iron Dominion. I'm here to count your crystal.`
  - Jumble: `[outraged sputtering pirate] A FINE? I'm a GHOST! I don't even have POCKETS!` (Pockets, somewhere, offended.)
- Clamp seals the lighthouse: "Inspection in progress." Drones guard the causeway. The old crystal is too weak to fight him, and the new one can't go in while he's there.

### 2. The town that's forgetting (morning into noon)
- **Honest Hal's Previously Plundered Goods.** His totals are wrong, always in his favor, and he blames the dimming. **Catch his sums** (multiples of 10, ×6 to ×9: "6 potions at 40 gold is not 260!") for a discount. He sells the first weapon upgrades and pet food.
- **The Salty Biscuit.** The baker's recipe book has gone blank. **Rebuild the biscuit recipe** from what the town still knows by heart (grams of flour, milliliters of milk). Biscuits become the healing item. Cade, rule forty: never say no to a biscuit.
- **Kit's shop hat.** At Hal's, Kit insists on replacing its hat at once. Hal sells it a "genuine admiral's hat, previously plundered" for 90 gold. It's a knitted tea cozy with a pom-pom. Catching Hal's sum gets it for 40. Kit wears it all chapter, with total dignity. `[deadpan] It's intimidating in a different way.` Wren calls Kit "Admiral Teapot" from then on.
- **Kit's hat hunt.** Pockets is seen in every place wearing two hats. Finding him is still the collectible, now with a chase line each time.
- Fiends in the streets: Fog Globs swallowing street signs (**prefixes and suffixes** bring the words back: "un-", "re-", "-ful"), Wick Moths around the dim lighthouse, Crumb Goblins raiding the café.

### 3. Tamsin (afternoon)
- A Caged Drone crashes in the market. Inside it is a frightened little fiend. Someone gets there first: a kid with a driftwood crook who pries the cage open and tells Cade not to burst it. "It's shy."
- **Tamsin Hollow,** about twelve, blunt, a walking book of fiend facts, much nicer to fiends than to people. She's been freeing what Clamp cages for a week.
- **Her hook:** she's the old lighthouse keeper's granddaughter. Her grandpa doesn't remember her name anymore, but he still climbs the lighthouse every night. She doesn't know why, and it scares her.
- **Wants:** proof that fiends can be good. **Fears:** that everything the town forgets (her creatures, her grandpa's memory) goes back to the dark.

### 4. The menagerie (afternoon)
- Her sanctuary is in the hold of a beached ship: tanks, nests and rebuilt fiends asleep in hammocks.
- **Remembering a fiend.** The Scrap Raptor that Cade captured back in chapter 1 (or a fresh one, if he never did) can be given its name back. **Puzzle:** feed and settle it on schedule ("It ate at 3:45. It's 4:20. How long?") and weigh its meal in grams. It hatches into **Rivet,** a dog-sized raptor with a tiny shield, who adores Cade at once. Cade writes Rivet's first **field note** (informative writing).
  - Cade: `[moved, then proudly] He has a shield. Kit, he has a shield.`
  - Kit: `[dryly] I see it. I'm choosing not to feel replaced.`
- **Tamsin joins.** Her first fight starts with her Overdrive full, as every hero's does.
- **Pockets is not a pet.** Tamsin tries to register him. He eats the form.

### 5. Tamsin in battle
- **Fights with time and measuring:** feeding schedules, tide times, a weighed and poured potion.
- **Counters fast fiends, a new kind only she can handle.** A fast fiend takes two turns every round and dodges most hits. Her **Slow** (an elapsed-time problem on a clock face: "Slow lasts from 4:10 to 4:35. How many minutes?") pins it to one turn and stops the dodging, so the others can land hits. Fast fiends this chapter: the Tick Mimic, Crumb Goblins and the Riptide Eel. Kit tells him the first time: "It's too quick for us. Tamsin, slow it down."
- **Her second command, Feed** (grams and liters): she weighs out a creature's meal or pours a tonic. It heals a hero, or calms a fiend so a ★★★ finish captures it. She's the only one who turns captures into pets.
- **Her Overdrive, Stampede:** chained quick problems, and every right answer sends one of her creatures charging across the field.
- **Her other job:** she can remember fiends the party captures with a ★★★ finish, turning them into pets for the heroes. That ability grows as the chapter goes on.

### 6. The way in (evening)
- The causeway is guarded. Tamsin knows the old smugglers' tunnel, which comes out under the cove's old wreck, but only at low tide.
- **Back to Shipwreck Cove:** the tide's out, and the sea cave is open for the first time. The first "go back, it's changed" reward: the cave, a new Jumble bottle on the sand (with a gloating new note), and a pet-food chest.
- **Puzzle:** the tide table. Low tide is at 8:15 and lasts 50 minutes, so when must they be out? (elapsed time). The tunnel runs under the harbor to the lighthouse's cellar.

### 7. The lamp room (night): the heart
- The lighthouse at night. The party climbs the stairs with the old keeper ahead of them, lantern in hand, not noticing them. He sits by the great lens, hums a few bars of a tune nobody in the harbor remembers, says goodnight to nothing, and goes back down.
- His **logbook** is on the desk. Most pages say only the date. Every page, in the same shaky hand, ends with the same line: *"Said goodnight to the bird."* The player reads it. Nobody explains it. Tamsin reads it over his shoulder, and she's quiet.
  - Tamsin: `[sadly] He forgot my name. He never forgot this.`
- Then the old crystal's last light catches the lens and throws a map of light across the ceiling: nodes and paths, one for each hero. **The Crystal Grid.**
  - Knox: `[in awe] A Sage chart. Every road a mind can walk.`
  - Kit: `[deadpan] It's a map. Of us. I'd like to see my node.`
- **The grid opens here.** Light from every fight since chapter 1 is waiting to spend, so the first visit is a shopping trip. From now on, every ambush pays out on it.
- Drones find them. They escape back through the tunnel. Something in the lens stirs and settles.

### 8. The Sword Master's dock (the second morning)
- The Sword Master has forgotten all his comebacks, and a Barnacle Brute has taken his chair. Win the dock back (a fight), and his **forge** opens: weapon upgrades with fiend parts and gold.
- A seed for later: he'll teach Wit Duels once he remembers his comebacks (chapter 3).

### 9. Gathering strength (the second day)
- The middle of the chapter is a loose day: the forge, the grid, Hal's shop and fiends to capture.
- **Tamsin's power grows.** She can now remember bigger fiends: a Volt Jelly for Wren, an Ink Slime for Knox (he's delighted: it writes), a tide creature for Maren.
- **Kit nudges when he's stuck.** It drops a stronger hint each time, then marks the trail on the map, so no one burns an hour hunting for a cave.

### 9b. Jumble's audit (the second afternoon): his serious moment
- Clamp boards the *Misspelled* to audit it. Jumble plays it big: insults, anagrams, "kuh-NIGHT". Clamp doesn't react. He reads from a file: *"Jumble. Sage school. Failed the final exam by one letter. Assessment: unremarkable."* He stamps it: **IRRELEVANT**.
- Jumble goes quiet. No joke. `[low, hurt pirate] It was one letter.` Clamp has already moved on.
- The party sees it from the dock. So does Knox. Hidden, he almost steps out, then doesn't. `[wistfully] He studied harder than anyone I knew.` (Their meeting stays saved for later.)
- A beat later Jumble is back to full volume, scrambling Clamp's paperwork. But the player has seen the person under the hat, and Cade, of all people, says it: `[earnestly] Nobody should be stamped irrelevant.`

### 10. Old places, changed (any time after beat 7)
- **The temple:** now that the crystal is nearly home, the Hall of Glyphs' tablets glow, and a sealed library room opens. Jumble's empty seat is in it, and a **crest map** for the first Legendary Weapon sits on the lectern.
- **The canyon:** the Albatross's lost parts (propeller, figurehead) turn up. Wren's rebuild starts toward the airship at winter break.
  - Wren: `[fondly] Hold on, old girl. I'm getting you back in one piece.`
- **The lair:** with the Geode Titan gone, a deeper glittering chamber and a hidden fight.
- **The grotto:** the crab still hums Maren's song, badly, but better.

### 11. The Drain Engine (the second dusk): the boss
- Clamp's inspection is "complete." The Drain Engine climbs out of the harbor on six iron legs and clamps onto the lighthouse. The lamp gutters, and the whole town forgets faster: the fiddler forgets which end to hold.
- The keeper climbs the stairs anyway, in the middle of it.
- **The fight, phase 1:** the Engine, with Clamp in his glass cockpit filling in forms ("Resisting inventory: section nine"). Tidebreaker can hurt it, but it keeps draining.
- **Phase 2:** the keeper reaches the lens and says, "Goodnight, bird," and it answers. Maren feels it: "There's a Titan in the light." **Binding Beaconwing:** he writes true words about the storm (writing), and Beaconwing unfolds out of the lens: beams for eyes, prisms for feathers, lightning in its wings. It tears the Engine off the tower.
- Clamp ejects, still filing. `[coldly] This isn't over. It's pending.`

### 12. The harbor remembers (night into the third morning): the ending
- The new crystal goes into the lens. Its light carries everything learned near it, and rolls over the harbor. The bells ring. The recipe book fills back in. Hal's sums come out right, and he's devastated.
- **The keeper remembers.** He looks at Tamsin and says her name. That moment needs no joke.
- **Kit's hat, back and gone again:** Pockets returns it... to Rivet's head. Kit: `[deadpan] Close.` Rivet hands it over. Kit puts it on, and for one line is whole again: `[warmly] Ah. Intimidating.` Then the bells ring, Beaconwing lifts out of the lens for a lap of the harbor before it joins Maren's Titans, and its downdraft blows the hat straight off Kit and onto Beaconwing's head as it flies away over the sea. Kit: `[deadpan] ...It suits him.` A beat. Kit puts the tea cozy back on.
- "Chapter 2 complete!" counts it up and reads back his words: Rivet's field note and the words that woke Beaconwing.
- **Jumble at night,** sulking that a villain with *paperwork* got all the attention. He sails for Smoke Mountain, which seeds chapter 3.
- To be continued.

## Maren's second song: the Lantern Song

Maren is the one the story turns on, the way the summoner is in FFX, so each chapter's heart runs through a song of hers.
- **Beat 7:** in the lamp room, the old keeper hums a few bars to the lens before he says goodnight. Maren freezes: it's a song. It's the harbor's old **Lantern Song**, which sailors sang to keep the light company. The town forgot it, and he has only the tune.
- **Beat 9:** she asks around the harbor. The baker remembers one word, and Hal remembers a line (and swears it was about him). She pieces it together. This time she isn't keeping an old song alive. She's making one whole again.
- **Beat 11:** in the boss fight's second phase, she stands beside the keeper and sings it to the lens. He joins in on the lines he knows. That's what wakes Beaconwing, together with his written words.
- **Beat 12:** it plays as the bells ring and the ending counts up.
- **Her arc:** her fear is that a song ends with her. Here she learns a song can be found again, which sets up bringing her own island's song home.
- **Production:** a new vocal track (like `music_maren_song`), plus a short hum for the keeper. The lyrics are below.

## The Lantern Song: lyrics (approved)

**The discovery:** the song's last line is *"Goodnight, bright bird, goodnight."* The keeper forgot the song, but its last line survived as a habit: he says goodnight to the bird every night and writes it in his log. What you know by heart, the dark takes last of all. Nobody says this out loud. Maren sings the last line, the keeper's lips move with it, and the player puts it together.

**Style:** the same voice and world as Maren's tide song (`music_maren_song`), so it's clearly hers. That song is a slow lullaby in a sea cave. This one is a sailors' song: a little steadier, with a slow walking rhythm like a rope being pulled, and it opens up into a big, bright chorus when Beaconwing wakes. It's simple enough that a kid can hum it after one listen.

---

### 1. The keeper's tune (heard in the lamp room, beat 7)
*The keeper hums alone, an old man's voice, wavering. No words.*

> Mm, mm-mm, mm...

### 2. The lantern (verse; Maren, beat 11)
*Solo voice and a soft concertina. A slow, steady sway.*

> Burn, little lantern, out over the sea,
> Bring every sail in the dark home to me.

### 3. The sails (Hal's line)
*The melody climbs. Low strings join.*

> Count every sail as the storm rolls by,
> Count them home, one by one.

### 4. The wake (chorus; when Beaconwing rises)
*Full and bright: strings, a low drum like distant thunder, and harbor bells on the last line.*

> Rise, bright bird, with the light in your wings,
> Sing for the sailors, and the old bell rings.

### 5. Goodnight (the last line; back to one voice)
*Everything falls away. Maren alone, gently, and the keeper's voice under hers on the last word.*

> Goodnight, bright bird, goodnight.

## How the new systems play

### Pets
- **Where they come from:** a fiend is a forgotten thing the dark got hold of. A fiend finished with a ★★★ hit is captured (that's already in the game). Tamsin can *remember* a capture: given its name back, it hatches into a small crystal creature, a companion for one hero.
- **In battle:** each hero brings one pet, which acts after a right answer (a nibble, a guard, a spark). Pet slots open on the Crystal Grid.
- **Evolving:** each pet is tied to its hero's skill and grows in three stages as that skill is mastered. At each stage he writes a field note on the new form (informative writing, December's ELA). Claude judges the detail, as in the Titan Forge, and a richer note gives the new form a stronger trait.
- **Chapter 2's pets:** Rivet for Cade (a Scrap Raptor with a tiny shield), a Volt Jelly for Wren, an Ink Slime for Knox (it writes) and a tide creature for Maren. New fiends add pet lines.
- **Pockets is not a pet.**

### The new fiends

| Fiend | Kind (who counters it) | The joke |
|---|---|---|
| **Barnacle Brute** | Armored (Cade) | A crab wearing someone's lost front door as a shell |
| **Wick Moth** | Flier (Wren) | Drawn to the fading lighthouse; very upset about it |
| **Fog Glob** | Slime (Knox) | Swallows signs, and the words on them go foggy |
| **Tick Mimic** | Fast (Tamsin) | A clock pretending to be a treasure chest, always seven minutes late |
| **Crumb Goblins** | Fast (Tamsin) | Raid the Salty Biscuit; small, many, crunchy |
| **Riptide Eel** | Fast (Tamsin) | Darts out of the dock's pilings, steals a potion and is gone before anyone's turn |
| **Caged Drones** | Flier, Dominion | Drones built around caged fiends. Freeing one is how Tamsin meets the party |
| **Boss: the Drain Engine** | Colossal (a Titan) | Clamp's crab-legged machine, clamped onto the lighthouse, drinking the crystal. Clamp drives it, filling in forms |

Every hero now has something only they do best: Cade armored fiends, Wren fliers, Knox slimes, Tamsin fast fiends, and Maren's Titans colossal ones.

### Getting unstuck
A playtester burned a long time looking for the Geode Titan's lair. So in chapter 2, Kit notices when he's been around a few places with no progress, and drops a stronger hint each time: a nudge, then a direction, then a glint on the spot, and finally the trail on the map. A "where next?" button in the pause menu does the same on demand. Places with nothing new left draw fewer fiends, and a grid node like FFX's No Encounters ("Calm Seas") earns quiet walking.

## The cast changes (for the story bible)

- **Tamsin Hollow, the Beastkeeper:** she wants proof fiends can be good, and she fears that what the town forgets goes back to the dark. Blunt, a fiend-facts nerd, nicknames nobody (the opposite of Wren). Tags that would suit her: `[flatly]`, `[delighted]`, `[matter-of-factly]`, `[protectively]`, and, rarely, `[sadly]`.
- **Inspector Clamp:** he wants everything counted, owned and filed. He fears disorder (and therefore Jumble). He's polite, precise and cold, and funny because he is completely serious. A real threat: he drains islands and doesn't care.
- **The old keeper:** he forgets everything except the bird. His want: to say goodnight.
- **Rivet:** Cade's raptor, loyal and very proud of its shield. No words, just clanks and happy chirps.
- **Beaconwing:** wants to be kept company. Fears the dark (the opposite of Tidebreaker, who lives in it).
